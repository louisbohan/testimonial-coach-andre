# Testimonial Review Checklist — Coach Andre

Run this on every submission before anything goes on the site. Takes about two minutes each.

---

## 1. Who made the diagnosis?

Read the testimonial looking for exactly one thing: who gets credit for naming the condition.

- **Doctor credited** → ✅ good. "My ortho diagnosed the tear, then Andre built around it."
- **No diagnosis mentioned** → ✅ good. Nothing to review.
- **Diagnosis credited to Andre** → ❌ rewrite. "Andre figured out my shoulder was torn." This is the line that causes trouble, even if the client wrote it in good faith.

**Default fix:** change the credit, not the sentiment. "Andre figured out my shoulder was torn" → "My doctor diagnosed the tear. Andre was the one who got me lifting again." Client's words stay true; the claim moves to the right person.

If the client insists Andre diagnosed them, do not publish. Thank them for the private feedback and quote the non-clinical half only.

---

## 2. Does it promise an outcome?

Training outcomes are fine ("I deadlift 135 now"). Medical promises are not ("fixed my herniated disc," "cured my sciatica," "no more medication").

- **Replace is fine:** "I can deadlift again" ✅
- **Cure is not:** "He cured my back" ❌

Swap cure/reverse/heal/fix for **helped / trained around / got me strong enough to**.

---

## 3. How specific is the health detail?

Not all health disclosure is equal. Rank what's in the testimonial:

- **No condition named** — "I came in with a bad back from a fall" → ✅ publish freely. It's an experience, not a record.
- **Condition named, no identifying details** — "my MS," "after my knee replacement," with first name + last initial only → ⚠️ acceptable **only** if the client picked *"Share, but keep health details out"*. If they picked plain "Share it," ask before publishing.
- **Condition + full name + photo** — 🚫 don't publish together. Pick two of the three.

---

## 4. Did they actually choose a consent level?

Check the Formspree submission for the `permission` field.

| Their choice | What you may publish |
|---|---|
| Share with my name and my words as written | Name, words, photo. Still run checks 1–2. |
| Share, but keep health details out | ✓ Words. ✗ Condition name. ✗ Photo if it identifies them in a medical context. |
| Ask me first before posting | Nothing until they answer. Reply, ask, screenshot their yes. |
| Private only | Nothing. Not a quote, not anonymized, not "a client said." Private means private. |

**If the field is missing or ambiguous, treat it as "Ask me first."** Never default to publish.

---

## 5. Would five of these together form a pattern?

Read the page as a whole, not testimonials one at a time. If three or four named people on the same page each have a health condition attached, the page itself makes a claim about Andre's practice — that he specializes in the diagnosed. He does, and that's the selling point. But it should come from *his* positioning copy, not from a wall of client disclosures.

**Rule of thumb:** at most **one testimonial per page** that names a condition alongside a full name. Rotate which one gets the spotlight; keep the rest to the "no condition named" tier.

---

## 6. Anything unverifiable?

- Years trained with him? Should match his own records roughly.
- Claimed credentials on Andre's behalf ("as a PT he...") → ❌ delete. He's a certified trainer, not a PT. Clients often get this wrong and it's a scope-of-practice claim made in his name.

---

## The 30-second version

1. Doctor credited for diagnosis? If no → rewrite the credit line.
2. Cure language? → swap for "helped / trained around."
3. Health detail + full name + photo at once? → cut one.
4. Consent field says they're fine with it? If unsure → ask.
5. Does the page now have more than one named person with a condition? → rotate one out.

---

*Practical guidance, not legal advice. If a testimonial creates doubt, the cheap move is always to quote less.*
